The CRI mechanism

CRI — Common Representative Intermediates — is a systematically reduced VOC-oxidation mechanism benchmarked against the near-explicit Master Chemical Mechanism.[3]

Why a reduced mechanism?

A global CTM cannot afford the Master Chemical Mechanism's ~13,000 species and ~50,000 reactions. The CRI construction method exploits a simple observation: a VOC's photochemical ozone-forming potential correlates with the number of reactive C–C and C–H bonds it contains.[3] MCM degradation intermediates are therefore lumped into a small set of generic peroxy radicals, carbonyls and nitrates indexed by that "reactive-bond" score — each lumped species is a common representative of a whole family of real intermediates. Part 1 of the CRI papers describes mechanism construction,[3] Part 2 the further reduction,[4] and Part 3 the secondary organic aerosol module.[6] The result reproduces MCM ozone production and PAN/SOA formation within a few percent at a fraction of the cost — about 5% of MCM's reactions.

Watch the lumping happen

Species names are real CRI names from the mechanism file.

The core photochemical engine

Sunlight photolyses O3 to O(1D), which reacts with water vapour to make OH — the atmosphere's detergent. OH converts CO and hydrocarbons to peroxy radicals (HO2, RO2), which oxidise NO to NO2; photolysis of NO2 then regenerates O3. Whether a parcel produces or destroys ozone depends on its NOx level and VOC loading — which is exactly what a parcel-resolved model captures well, since each parcel carries its own emission and chemistry history. Try the interactive cycle explorer to see the coupled reactions, and the night-time switch to see how the photolysis channels switch off.

Photolysis

Photolysis rates are computed inside the model from the local solar zenith angle, an ozone column (built per grid column), cloud fields and prescribed cross-sections/quantum yields — giving a realistic diurnal cycle in every parcel rather than a prescribed J-value climatology. About 110 photolysable channels are represented in the current build, from J(O3→O(1D)) and J(NO2) to the many carbonyl splittings that drive VOC degradation.

Nitrogen reservoirs: PAN, RO2NO2, HNO3

Acyl peroxy nitrates (PAN and its analogues) sequester NOx at cold temperatures and release it on descent into warm air, ventilating pollution to the remote troposphere — STOCHEM-CRI reproduces the observed PAN budget well in the lower troposphere.[11] The extended mechanism adds non-acyl peroxy nitrates (RO2NO2): 45 species including CH3O2NO2 whose short thermal lifetimes confine them to the cold upper troposphere, where they act as an additional NOx reservoir and modestly raise global O3, OH and NO3.[7] HNO3 is the main terminal sink, removed by dry and wet deposition.

Secondary organic aerosol

The CRI Part-3 module tracks semi-volatile oxidation products (peroxides, acids, nitrates) that partition reversibly into an absorbing organic aerosol phase — in the code, gas-phase precursors such as RCOOH25 equilibrate with particle-phase partners (the P#### species).[6] Particles and their precursors are both advected, so SOA forms downwind of VOC sources with realistic timescales.

Radical bookkeeping

STOCHEM-CRI has also been used to study the global RO2 distribution — dominated by CH3O2 (~83%), with acyl peroxy and isoprene-/terpene-derived radicals contributing most of the rest — and to test the proposed role of RO2·H2O complexes in perturbing HOx cycling.[8] Budget diagnostics written to .bud and .tot let production and loss terms be closed species-by-species.
