The mathematics

Every component of STOCHEM is a recognisable object from applied mathematics: characteristics of a conservation law, Runge–Kutta integration, Itô diffusion, Bernoulli sampling, a stiff ODE solved by linearised implicit iteration, and a Poisson census. This page collects them.

1. From a conservation law to particles

A mixing ratio c(x,t) transported by the wind obeys the advection equation with sources:

∂c/∂t + u·∇c = S(c, x, t)

The method of characteristics says: along any curve x(t) satisfying

dx/dt = u(x, t)

the PDE collapses to an ordinary differential equation for the material derivative:

Dc/Dt ≡ ∂c/∂t + u·∇c = S

A Lagrangian model is just a discretisation of the characteristic family: instead of solving the PDE on a mesh, STOCHEM integrates the ODE pair along 50,048 characteristic curves — its air parcels.[1] Three consequences fall out for free:

  • No CFL condition on advection — there is no grid Courant number; the step is limited only by trajectory accuracy, hence the 3-hour ASTEP.
  • No numerical diffusion and no negative concentrations — nothing is fluxed between boxes, so nothing can be oversmeared or driven negative.
  • Exact mass conservation — parcels are constant mass; the global tracer integral changes only through S, never through transport error.

2. The trajectory: fourth-order Runge–Kutta

Each parcel's position is advanced over the 3-hour step by classical RK4 (subroutine RKO4), with the wind field interpolated in space and time to each trial position by VELOC/INTERP:

k₁ = u(x, t)  ·  k₂ = u(x + Δt·k₁/2, t + Δt/2)
k₃ = u(x + Δt·k₂/2, t + Δt/2)  ·  k₄ = u(x + Δt·k₃, t + Δt)
xⁿ⁺¹ = xⁿ + (Δt/6)(k₁ + 2k₂ + 2k₃ + k₄)

Local error is O(Δt⁵); at Δt = 3 h against 6-hourly analysed winds the dominant error is the temporal interpolation of u, not the integrator — which is why ASTEP is chosen to match the met frequency rather than any stability limit.

3. The random walk that is diffusion

A parcel following only the resolved wind would never mix with its neighbours. STOCHEM adds the missing turbulence as an Itô stochastic differential equation:

dx = u(x,t) dt + √(2K) dW

discretised in VELOC as an Euler–Maruyama kick added to the deterministic velocity, S·√(2K/ASTEP) with S ~ N(0,1):

Δx = u Δt + √(2KΔt)·N(0,1)

With HDIFF = 5300 m² s⁻¹ and Δt = 10,800 s, the rms horizontal displacement per step is √(2·5300·10800) ≈ 10.7 km — switched off near the poles where the metric term dominates. The key identity: the Fokker–Planck equation of this SDE is

∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(uρ) = K ∇²ρ

i.e. the ensemble of stochastic parcels solves the same advection–diffusion equation an Eulerian model solves explicitly — but by Monte Carlo rather than by finite differences.[2] The price is statistical noise; the payoff is zero numerical diffusion.

4. Convection as Bernoulli sampling

The CLMIX scheme computes a convective fraction per met column — from the precipitation rate via a mass-flux estimate (MFLUX = ACP·Δt / (PEFF·q)) or 4× cloud cover for non-precipitating cloud — then draws an independent Bernoulli trial for each parcel below cloud top:

X ~ Bernoulli(FRAC),   entrained if R < FRAC

Entrained parcels in a cell are all set to the group mean c̄ = (1/N_e)Σ cᵢ. Two properties worth noting:

  • Mass is conserved exactly. After mixing, the sum over the entrained set is N_e·c̄ = Σcᵢ — the pre-mixing sum. No mass is created or destroyed, only redistributed.
  • Convergence is geometric. Each event retains a fraction (1 − FRAC) of parcels unmixed, so column-mean deviations decay roughly like (1 − FRAC) per event — the column relaxes exponentially toward homogeneity at a rate set by the convection intensity.[10]

5. The chemistry: a stiff ODE, and how it is actually solved

Inside each parcel the CRI mechanism is a coupled system

dc/dt = f(c) = P(c) − L(c)·c,   c ∈ ℝ²⁴¹

where P collects all production terms and L the (pseudo-)first-order loss coefficients. It is violently stiff: radical lifetimes are ~10⁻³ s while CH₄ lives ~10 years — a span of ~10¹¹ in eigenvalues — so explicit schemes would need impossibly small steps. STOCHEM does not use a general stiff solver. Instead, each 3-hour advection step is divided into DTS = 300 s chemistry substeps, and each substep applies a linearised implicit (quasi-analytic) update:[1]

cⁿ⁺¹ = (cⁿ + Δt·P) / (1 + Δt·L)

iterated as a Picard fixed point — NIT = 8 iterations in DERIV, with P and L re-evaluated at the current iterate each pass. The formula is the exact solution of the linearised ODE dc/dt = P − Lc with frozen coefficients — unconditionally positive, stable for any Δt, and exact in the limits Δt·L → 0 (Euler) and t → ∞ (steady state c = P/L). Rate constants come from CHEMCO in Arrhenius form k = A·e^(−E/RT), equilibria and Henry's-law constants from EQMCON.

6. Photolysis: an integral over the spectrum

Each photolysis rate is the spectral integral

J = ∫ σ(λ,T)·φ(λ)·F(λ,χ, z, O₃) dλ

of absorption cross-section σ × quantum yield φ × actinic flux F, which depends on solar zenith angle χ, altitude and overhead ozone. Evaluating the integral at every parcel every step would dominate the run time, so PHOT tabulates J on a grid of χ/level/O₃-column and JVALS interpolates — four evaluations per advection step, with DJCALC interpolating within each 300 s chemistry substep.[1] The day/night asymmetry this produces is what drives the diurnal cycle shown on the chemistry page.

7. Sources and sinks

Emissions appear as inhomogeneous terms: a cell emitting E molecules per step adds E/N_c to each of its N_c parcels (EGET) — mass-preserving by construction. Sinks are folded into the loss operator L:

dry deposition: L_dry = v_d / H_BL   ·   wet scavenging: L_wet = Λ(P)

— a boundary-layer ventilation timescale for the first, a precipitation-dependent scavenging coefficient for the second (DDCALC, DWCALC).

8. Operator splitting

The full step is a composition of single-physics maps —

cⁿ⁺¹ = S_dep ∘ S_chem ∘ S_mix ∘ S_adv cⁿ

a first-order (Lie) splitting with O(Δt) splitting error. Each factor is cheap or exact in its own frame — advection is the trajectory integral, chemistry the implicit iteration above, mixing the Bernoulli draw — and the small splitting error is the accepted price for never having to solve one coupled monster system.[1]

9. Output: the census is a Poisson process

A grid-cell mean is a sample mean over whatever parcels happen to be in the cell: c̄_cell = (1/N_c) Σ cᵢ. If parcels were Poisson distributed, N_c ~ Poisson(μ) gives count noise

σ/μ = 1/√μ

With 50,048 parcels over 23,328 cells, μ ≈ 2.1 — so even a perfectly-mixed field carries ~70% cell-to-cell sampling noise before any chemistry. This is why burdens and zonal means (which average many cells) are well-converged while single-cell fields are not — try it live in the census demo.

10. Putting it together

One advection step of STOCHEM is: integrate the characteristic ODE by RK4 (§2), apply the diffusion SDE kick (§3), inject source terms and first-order sinks (§7), advance the stiff chemistry by 8×-iterated quasi-analytic substeps (§5) with interpolated photolysis (§6), Bernoulli-homogenise cloudy columns (§4), then bin the ensemble into Eulerian cell means (§9). Repeat 8 times a day, ~3,000 times a year.
